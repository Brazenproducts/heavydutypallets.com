Heavy Duty Pallets for Freezer and Cold-Chain Operations

Heavydutypallets Editorial • October 2026

Cold-chain and freezer operations punish pallets in ways ambient warehouses never do. Temperatures that swing from blast-freezer lows to dock-level condensation, repeated wash-downs, and strict food-safety audits all expose the weaknesses of traditional wood. For procurement teams managing frozen protein, produce, or pharmaceutical cold storage, heavy duty plastic pallets have become the default specification. This guide explains the performance factors that matter when you source pallets for low-temperature environments.

Cold Embrittlement and Material Choice

Not all plastics behave the same at freezer temperatures. Standard polypropylene can become brittle well below freezing, so pallets destined for blast freezers should use grades formulated or reinforced for low-temperature impact resistance. Ask any supplier for the rated minimum operating temperature and the drop-test performance at that temperature, not just at room conditions. The heavy duty PP pallets from Elipacko can be specified with reinforcement and resin selection suited to cold-chain duty, which prevents the corner cracking that plagues underspecified pallets after a few freeze cycles.

Moisture: The Hidden Cost of Wood

Wood pallets absorb moisture, and in cold-chain operations that moisture becomes a cascade of problems. Water adds weight, which inflates freight cost on every shipment. It freezes into ice that unbalances loads and jams automated handling. As pallets thaw at the dock, they shed water onto floors and products, creating slip hazards and contamination risk. Plastic pallets absorb no moisture, so their weight stays constant, their dimensions stay stable, and they do not carry water into clean rooms or freezers.

Hygiene and Audit Readiness

Frozen food and pharmaceutical facilities operate under HACCP plans and third-party audits that scrutinize every surface touching product. Wood harbors bacteria in cracks, retains residues, and cannot be reliably sanitized. Closed-deck plastic pallets wash clean, dry quickly, and present no splinters or protruding nails. For regulated cold-chain work, pallets molded from FDA-compliant resin let buyers document material compliance directly, simplifying audit preparation. This documentation advantage is often decisive when quality teams, not just logistics, weigh in on the purchase.

  • No moisture absorption or weight gain
  • No splinters, nails, or harborage points
  • Washable, sanitizable closed decks
  • FDA-compliant resin options for traceability

Throughput and Automation in the Cold

Cold-storage facilities increasingly run automated storage and retrieval systems to limit the time workers spend in sub-zero aisles. Automation demands dimensional consistency that wood cannot hold once it absorbs moisture and warps. Molded plastic pallets maintain tight tolerances trip after trip, reducing conveyor jams and retrieval errors in exactly the environment where a stoppage is most expensive to clear. When evaluating the full Elipacko industrial packaging line, confirm the dimensional tolerance and whether the deck and runner design suits your conveyor entry direction.

Total Cost Over the Cold-Chain Lifecycle

Plastic pallets carry a higher unit price than wood, but cold-chain economics favor them over a multi-year horizon. Eliminated moisture weight lowers freight. Longer service life spreads the purchase across hundreds of trips. Reduced audit findings and contamination incidents avoid costs that rarely appear on a pallet quote but dominate the real budget. Building a cost-per-trip model across the expected service life almost always closes the gap and then favors plastic for demanding freezer duty.

Sourcing for Cold-Chain Specifications

Because cold-chain pallets are frequently semi-custom—specific footprints, reinforcement levels, and resin grades—working with a direct manufacturer gives you engineering input and removes distributor markup. Define your minimum operating temperature, racking load, footprint, and compliance needs up front so the manufacturer can quote the correct cold-chain configuration the first time.
