FDA & USDA Compliance Guide for Plastic Pallets

Heavydutypallets Editorial • October 2026

Why Compliance Matters for Pallets

In food, meat, poultry, dairy, and pharmaceutical operations, the pallet is a direct part of the hygienic chain. A pallet that enters a wash-down zone, a cold room, or a USDA-inspected processing area must not shed fibers, harbor bacteria, or introduce chemical contaminants. Wood pallets struggle here: they absorb moisture, splinter, accept nail fasteners that corrode, and can carry mold or pest infestation. This is why so many processors have migrated to polypropylene pallets that can be documented, cleaned, and audited.

Compliance is not a single certificate. It is a combination of material selection, manufacturing controls, and the supporting documentation that lets your quality team pass an inspection. Understanding each layer helps procurement specify correctly the first time.

The FDA Resin Requirement

The foundational requirement is that any surface contacting food be made from food-contact-safe resin. Virgin polypropylene compounded to meet FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 is the standard specification for food-grade pallets. This matters because recycled or regrind material of unknown origin may contain contaminants, colorants, or additives that are not cleared for food contact. When sourcing, always confirm the resin grade in writing. USDA Considerations for Meat and Poultry

USDA-inspected facilities add another layer. Equipment and material-handling surfaces in these plants must withstand aggressive sanitation cycles, including hot water, caustic detergents, and sanitizers, without degrading. Non-porous polypropylene is ideal because it does not absorb these chemicals or the organic matter they are removing. A smooth, sealed pallet surface with rounded edges and open, drainable designs prevents standing water and the biofilm it encourages.

For meat and poultry plants, also evaluate whether the pallet design avoids closed cavities where blood, fat, or wash water can accumulate. Nestable and open-deck geometries that drain fully and dry quickly are strongly preferred over designs with enclosed internal voids.

Documentation Your Auditor Will Ask For

Specifying a compliant pallet is only half the task; you must prove it. Before an audit, assemble the following from your supplier:

  • A material declaration identifying the resin grade and FDA food-contact compliance.
  • A statement of manufacturing location and quality controls.
  • Load rating and dimensional specifications for the exact model in use.
  • Cleaning and sanitation guidance validated for the material.

A Practical Buyer Checklist

When you next specify pallets for a regulated environment, confirm: food-grade resin to 21 CFR 177.1520, a drainable and cleanable geometry, a published and verified load rating, documented origin, and available cleaning validation. Request custom dimensions where stock sizes waste rack space or create overhang. By treating compliance as a documented specification rather than an afterthought, your procurement team protects both product safety and audit readiness while controlling total cost.
