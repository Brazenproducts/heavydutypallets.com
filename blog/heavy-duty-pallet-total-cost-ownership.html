Total Cost of Ownership for Heavy Duty Pallets: Beyond Unit Price

Heavydutypallets Editorial • October 2026

Unit Price Is the Wrong Starting Point

Procurement teams under budget pressure often default to the lowest sticker price when buying pallets. For heavy duty applications, that approach almost always costs more over time. A durable plastic pallet may carry two to four times the unit price of a stringer wood pallet, yet deliver a far lower cost per use once lifespan, damage, labor, and disposal are counted. Building a total cost of ownership (TCO) model turns pallet purchasing from a line-item decision into a measurable savings opportunity.

This article gives logistics and sourcing professionals a practical framework to compare heavy duty pallet options on true cost, not just acquisition price.

The Five Cost Drivers to Model

A credible TCO model captures five categories. Collect real data from your operation for each one rather than relying on vendor averages.

  • Acquisition cost: unit price plus freight and any tooling for custom sizes.
  • Useful life: number of trips or years before retirement. Heavy duty plastic pallets commonly reach 10x the trip count of wood in closed loops.
  • Damage and product loss: cost of goods damaged by broken boards, protruding nails, or splinters, plus rejected shipments.
  • Labor and handling: time spent on repair, sorting, nail removal, and handling inconsistent wood dimensions in automation.
  • Disposal and compliance: fees to dispose of broken wood, ISPM-15 treatment for export, and sanitation costs in food plants.

Calculating Cost Per Trip

The single most useful TCO metric is cost per trip. Divide the fully loaded lifetime cost of a pallet by the number of trips it completes. A wood pallet at a low unit price that lasts a handful of trips and generates repair and damage costs often lands at a higher cost per trip than a plastic pallet that completes hundreds of cycles with near-zero maintenance.

In closed-loop systems where pallets return to your facility, plastic's advantage compounds because you capture the full lifespan. In one-way export shipping the math shifts, which is why many operations run a hybrid fleet: premium reusable pallets internally and lower-cost options for one-way lanes. Hidden Costs That Favor Plastic

Several costs rarely appear on a purchase order but dominate real spend. Automated lines jam on inconsistent wood dimensions, triggering downtime far more expensive than any pallet. Food and pharma plants spend heavily on pest control and sanitation that plastic eliminates because it does not absorb moisture or harbor contaminants. Worker injuries from nails and splinters carry claims costs and lost time. Each of these belongs in the model, and each tilts the result toward durable plastic.

  • Downtime from automation jams.
  • Sanitation and pest control in regulated environments.
  • Injury claims from damaged wood.
  • Weight-driven freight differences on export loads.

Building and Defending the Business Case

Present TCO as cost per trip over a defined horizon, typically three to five years, and include a sensitivity analysis on lifespan assumptions. Conservative estimates still favor durable plastic in most closed-loop operations, which strengthens approval odds with finance.
