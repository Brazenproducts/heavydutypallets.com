Heavy Duty Pallets for Automation: Conveyor and AS/RS Compatibility

Heavydutypallets Editorial • October 2026

Automation Raises the Bar on Pallet Specifications

In a manual forklift operation, a pallet that is a quarter inch out of square or a few pounds heavier than its neighbor rarely causes a problem. In an automated facility, those same variances stop a line. Automated storage and retrieval systems, powered conveyors, and robotic palletizers are engineered to tight tolerances, and the pallet becomes a moving machine component rather than a passive platform. This changes how procurement must write a heavy duty pallet specification.

This guide covers the pallet attributes that matter most when you feed equipment rather than people. Operations standardizing on automation-ready units can review the heavy duty PP pallets from Elipacko, which are molded to consistent tolerances suited to conveyor and ASRS handling.

Dimensional Consistency Is Non-Negotiable

Wood pallets vary from unit to unit because they are assembled from natural material. Plastic pallets are injection or structural-foam molded, so every unit comes out of the same tool with repeatable dimensions. For automation, this repeatability is the single biggest advantage of switching to plastic. Conveyor guide rails, ASRS shuttles, and robotic end effectors all assume a pallet that lands within a narrow dimensional window.

  • Length and width tolerance should be confirmed against your equipment supplier's intake specification.
  • Diagonal squareness keeps the pallet tracking straight through transfers.
  • Deck height consistency prevents hang-ups at elevation changes.

Bottom Design Drives Conveyability

The underside of the pallet is where automation compatibility is won or lost. Powered roller and chain conveyors require specific bottom geometry to track reliably. A pallet with the wrong runner orientation or inadequate bottom-board coverage will skew, jam, or stall. When specifying for conveyor use, document the conveyor type and ask the manufacturer to confirm the bottom design is validated for that transport method.

Some bottom patterns run cleanly in one conveyor direction but not the other, so confirm the intended travel orientation. For bidirectional systems, a symmetrical bottom pattern is worth the premium.

Weight Consistency and Repeatability

Robotic palletizers calibrate lift and placement partly around expected pallet weight. Large swings in unit weight force conservative calibration that slows throughput. Molded plastic pallets hold tight weight tolerances because they are made from a controlled resin charge, unlike wood pallets that absorb moisture and gain weight unpredictably. This consistency pays off in faster, more confident robotic cycles.

Durability Under Repeated Machine Cycles

Automation subjects pallets to constant transfers, stops, and accelerations. A pallet that chips, sheds splinters, or loses fasteners introduces debris that can foul sensors and jam mechanisms. Single-piece molded plastic pallets have no nails or loose boards, which eliminates the most common source of automation debris. Over thousands of cycles, this reliability translates directly into uptime.

Load Rating Still Matters

Automation compatibility does not replace structural requirements. You still need the correct racking and dynamic load ratings for your loads and storage configuration. Verify that the automation-ready pallet you choose also meets the rackable rating for your ASRS rack depth, since deep-lane storage places demanding spans on the pallet underside.

Procurement Checklist for Automation-Ready Pallets

  • Obtain your equipment supplier's pallet intake specification before sourcing.
  • Confirm dimensional and squareness tolerances in writing.
  • Match bottom geometry to your exact conveyor type and travel direction.
  • Verify weight consistency for robotic palletizer calibration.
  • Validate racking load ratings for ASRS rack depth.
  • Run a pilot batch through live equipment before full rollout.
